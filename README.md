# Whitehead and Russell, *Principia Mathematica*, in Lean 4

*Principia Mathematica*, by Alfred North Whitehead and Bertrand Russell, Volume I (1910) and Volume II (1912) ([Project Gutenberg #78050](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/78050) and [#78255](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/78255), public domain in the USA), formalized in Lean 4 and mathlib.

Every numbered proposition of the two volumes, from ✱1·01 to ✱234·73, is a Lean declaration. There are 7355 of them. Each one quotes the formula as printed, states it in Lean, and proves it. Where the printed formula admits more than one reading, the docstring says which one is taken and why. Where it is false, the docstring says why, and the corrected statement is proved. In most such cases the failure of the printed statement is proved as well. The mathematical claims of the Introduction, the Summaries of Parts I and II, the Note to Section D, the Prefatory Statement and the General Note on Cardinal Correlators are formalized as well.

## Highlights

- **1 + 1 = 2.** ✱54·43 (Vol. I, p. 379) is `pm54_43` in [`Part2/SectionA.lean`](PrincipiaMathematica/Part2/SectionA.lean): if `α` and `β` are unit classes, they are disjoint if and only if `α ∪ β ∈ 2`. PM remarks that 1 + 1 = 2 will follow from it once arithmetical addition is defined. That happens in Volume II: ✱110·643, `1 +_c 1 = 2`, is `pm110_643` in [`Part3/SectionB.lean`](PrincipiaMathematica/Part3/SectionB.lean).
- **The theory of deduction, ✱1–✱5**, in [`Part1/SectionA.lean`](PrincipiaMathematica/Part1/SectionA.lean). PM's primitive ideas `∼` and `∨` become Lean's connectives. Its definitions of `⊃`, `.` and `≡` (✱1·01, ✱3·01, ✱4·01) become theorems. PM's rules of inference (✱1·1, ✱1·11) are recorded as derivable rules. [`Basic.lean`](PrincipiaMathematica/Basic.lean) gives the rules for reading PM's dots as brackets, with worked examples.
- **The ancestral and induction.** ✱90 defines the ancestral `R_*` through hereditary classes, and ✱90·112 is the principle of induction along `R`. ✱91–✱97 cover powers of a relation, posterity and families. ✱120 applies the same principle to the inductive cardinals (✱120·13).
- **Cardinal arithmetic, Part III.** Cardinals are classes of similar classes, taken in explicit types. This part covers sums, products and powers, commutativity (✱110·51, ✱113·27) and Cantor's theorem `Nc'Cl'α > Nc'α` (✱117·66). It also covers inductive and reflexive cardinals, `ℵ₀` and the axiom of infinity (✱118–✱126). The Schröder–Bernstein theorem is ✱73·88 in Part II. The type conventions of Volume II's Prefatory Statement (IT, IIT, AT) are realized by explicit type parameters and hypotheses. Each use is cited in a status line.
- **Relation-arithmetic, Part IV.** Ordinal similarity, relation-numbers, sums and products of relations, the principle of first differences, the associative law of relational multiplication (✱174) and powers of relation-numbers (✱186).
- **Series, Part V.** Transitive and connected relations, maxima, minima, sequent points and limits (✱200–✱208); sections, segments and Dedekindian relations (✱211, ✱214); convergence and continuity of functions (✱230–✱234).
- **The Introduction** ([`Introduction.lean`](PrincipiaMathematica/Introduction.lean), 44 units). Russell's paradox is `Intro.russell`: in a single type with a membership relation, no class contains exactly the classes that are not members of themselves. The Burali-Forti contradiction is resolved as PM resolves it. The ordinal of all ordinals of type `u` is `Ordinal.univ.{u, u+1}`, of the next type, and it exceeds every one of them (`Intro.lt_ordinal_of_all_ordinals`). The liar, Richard's paradox and the greatest cardinal are treated too.

## Status

Every proposition `✱N·M` is the declaration `pmN_M`. Its docstring gives the formula as printed.

| Status | Propositions |
|---|---|
| proved | 6083 |
| proved (reading): proved under a stated reading (a Lean primitive for a PM definition, a type convention, an erratum or a transcription error of the e-text, or a choice between readings); the docstring explains it | 824 |
| definition: a Df, with its defining equation proved | 344 |
| proved (corrected): false as printed; the corrected statement is proved, and for 74 of them a counterexample to the printed one | 87 |
| remark: a rule about notation or types, with no mathematical content to prove | 17 |
| **total** | **7355**, in 135 numbers, with no `sorry` |

- Every declaration depends only on Lean's standard axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. This is checked by `scripts/AxiomsAudit.lean`.
- The number-by-number map, with each number's title, volume and file, is [docs/PROPOSITIONS.md](docs/PROPOSITIONS.md). It is generated by `scripts/prop_table.py`.
- The Introduction's 44 units are not counted in the table. Their declarations are in the namespace `PrincipiaMathematica.Intro`.

**Some of PM's errors** (each is a `proved (corrected)` proposition, and each counterexample is proved as `pmN_M_printed_false`; all 87 are in [docs/FAITHFULNESS.md](docs/FAITHFULNESS.md)):
- **✱24·431**, a law of the class calculus. The right side as printed is not equal to the left side: take `α = γ = V` and `β = Λ`. The errata do not list it. The intended right side is that of ✱24·432.
- **✱117·632**, a step towards ✱117·633: the product of the cardinals of the members of `κ` is at least their sum. The relation `T` that PM defines is not one-one. A one-one `T` with the required domain and converse domain exists, and that is what ✱117·633 uses.
- **✱160·35**, a monotonicity law for the sum of relations. As printed it is false (`P = R = S = Λ̇`, `Q = V̇`). The proof PM cites gives `P ⤉ R ⊂̇ Q ⤉ S`.
- **✱186·3**, powers of relation-numbers. Base and exponent are interchanged; ✱185·29, which PM cites, gives the other order.
- **✱202·54**, connected relations. The hypothesis is that `P⥏α` is connected, but the proof uses that `P` is connected. With `P` relating `0` to `1` and `1` to `2`, and `α = {0, 2}`, the printed statement fails. The same slip recurs in ✱202·55, ✱205·261 and ✱205·91.
- **✱174·161 and its consequences.** The associative law of relational multiplication fails when the null relation `Λ̇` is in the field of `P`, which PM's hypothesis allows. A hypothesis that excludes this case is added to 22 propositions of ✱174–✱185.

## Reading a proposition

```lean
/-- ✱110·643. \(\vdash .1 +_{\text{c}} 1=2\)

(Constant formal numbers under IIT.) The two `1`s are arithmetical occurrences (IT does not bind
them): each has its own type, with the IIT hypothesis `∃!1` (`[Nonempty W]`); the equation is in
a free type `γ` (in which `2` may be `Λ`, and then so is `1 +_c 1`).

**Status.** proved (reading): convention IIT (Prefatory Statement, IV): the 1s are taken in types
in which they exist (PM assumes every type has a member, Introduction, Ch. I) -/
theorem pm110_643 {W₁ W₂ : Type*} [Nonempty W₁] [Nonempty W₂] :
    ((one : Set (Set W₁)) +ᶜ (one : Set (Set W₂)) : Set (Set γ)) = two := by
```

Each starred number `✱N` also has a unit block `## [✱N] Title`. It quotes the opening of PM's summary and has a **Lean reading** paragraph and a **Status** line. Shared conventions are in [`PrincipiaMathematica/Basic.lean`](PrincipiaMathematica/Basic.lean):
- PM's connectives are Lean's, with classical logic; the dots are read as brackets (§2);
- individuals of a type are `α : Type u`, a class is `Set α`, and a relation is `α → β → Prop`; typical ambiguity is type polymorphism;
- a description `(℩x)(φx)` is never a Lean term: `ψ(℩x)(φx)` is `TheIs φ ψ`, and `ψ(R'y)` is `Desc R y ψ`;
- PM's `⊂` is `⊆`, and PM's `∃!α` ("`α` has members") is `A.Nonempty`;
- reducibility (✱12·1) and the multiplicative axiom (✱88·03) are provable in Lean. `Mult ax` is still kept as a hypothesis wherever PM has it. The axiom of infinity is not provable, and it stays a hypothesis.

## Building

Lean v4.36.0 and mathlib v4.36.0 are pinned in `lean-toolchain` and `lakefile.toml`.

```sh
lake exe cache get                       # download prebuilt mathlib
lake build                               # every file
lake env lean scripts/AxiomsAudit.lean   # axiom audit
python3 scripts/prop_table.py --check    # regenerate docs/PROPOSITIONS.md and check statuses
```

**Memory:** the Section files are large (up to about 500 kB each), and `lake build` builds as many files at once as the machine has CPU threads. On a machine with many cores and less than about 32 GB of RAM, build a few modules at a time (`lake build PrincipiaMathematica.Part1.SectionA`, then `lake build PrincipiaMathematica.Part1.SectionB PrincipiaMathematica.Part1.SectionC`, …). Or check one file with `python3 scripts/check.py PrincipiaMathematica/Part1/SectionA.lean`; it limits how many Lean processes run at once (`--slots`).

On Windows, keep the repo at a short path such as `D:\Projects\principia-mathematica-lean`, because of the 260-character path limit.

## Volume III

Volume III (1913) contains the end of Part V (well-ordered series, finite and infinite series and ordinals, compact series, rational series and continuous series) and Part VI, Quantity. It is not yet on Project Gutenberg. It is being prepared at Distributed Proofreaders, and it will be formalized here in the same way once its Gutenberg edition is published. See [docs/SOURCES.md](docs/SOURCES.md).

## Contributing

Lean checks the proofs, not whether each statement says what Whitehead and Russell meant. If you find a misreading, or a proposition marked `proved` or `proved (reading)` that should be `proved (corrected)`, please open an issue.
