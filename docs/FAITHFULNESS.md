# Faithfulness: how *Principia Mathematica* is read

Lean checks the proofs. This file is about the other half: whether each statement says what Whitehead and Russell say. Every numbered proposition ✱N·M is the declaration `pmN_M`. Its docstring gives the formula as printed, copied from `data/principia.json`. When the proposition is not plainly `proved`, the docstring ends with a **Status.** line that gives the reason. Every number ✱N has a unit block `## [✱N] Title` with a **Lean reading** and a **Status**. This file collects the conventions that apply throughout, every correction, the kinds of reading, the errata, and what is not formalized.

## Conventions

The conventions are fixed in [`PrincipiaMathematica/Basic.lean`](../PrincipiaMathematica/Basic.lean) (§1–§10) and extended in the module headers of `Defs/Part1.lean` to `Defs/Part5.lean`. The symbol table from PM to Lean is in Basic §7, and `Defs/Part2.lean` to `Defs/Part5.lean` each extend it.

### Logic (✱1–✱14)

- **Propositions are `Prop`.** `∼p`, `p ∨ q`, `p ⊃ q`, `p . q`, `p ≡ q` are `¬p`, `p ∨ q`, `p → q`, `p ∧ q`, `p ↔ q`. Why: these are the connectives PM's primitive ideas describe. The Dfs ✱1·01, ✱3·01, ✱4·01 become theorems that Lean's connective agrees with PM's definiens (classical logic).
- **The assertion-sign.** `⊢ . p` is "`p` is a Lean theorem". Why: this is what assertion means in PM.
- **Elementary propositions.** The variables of ✱1–✱5 are arbitrary `p q r : Prop`. Why: Lean's `Prop` is one impredicative type of all propositions, so the extension to every order in ✱9 is automatic.
- **Rules of inference.** ✱1·1, ✱1·11, ✱9·12, ✱9·13 and their analogues are stated as the derivable rules (`pm1_1 : p → (p → q) → q`). Why: PM says they cannot be expressed in its symbolism; in Lean, inference is applying a proof.
- **Dots.** PM's dots are read first, and give the bracketing: Group I dots at `⊃`, `≡`, `∨`, `= Df`; Group II after a quantifier prefix; Group III for logical product. A bracket stops at a larger number of dots, or at an equal number of an equal or stronger group. Why: this is the rule of the Introduction, Ch. I. The worked examples are in Basic §2.
- **Chains.** `p ⊃ q ⊃ r` is `(p → q) ∧ (q → r)`, not `p → q → r` (✱3·02). `x = y = z` and every chain of `=`, `≡`, `⊂` is the conjunction of its links (✱13·03, ✱4·02). Flat sums and products are bracketed to the left (✱2·33, ✱4·34). Why: these are PM's own Dfs.
- **Hypotheses.** `p . q . ⊃ . r` is `p ∧ q → r`, kept uncurried. Why: the statement then has PM's shape.
- **Real and apparent variables.** `(x) . φx` is `∀ x, φ x` and `(∃x) . φx` is `∃ x, φ x`. A real variable (a free letter of an asserted formula) is an argument of the theorem, usually implicit. `φx ⊃_x ψx` is `∀ x, φ x → ψ x` (✱10·02). Why: PM's "ambiguous assertion" of `φx` asserts it for every value of `x`.
- **Identity.** PM's `=` is Lean's `Eq`. PM's definiens `(φ) : φ!x . ⊃ . φ!y` is `PMIdent`, and `pm13_01` proves `x = y ↔ PMIdent x y`. Why: the two agree, and Lean's `=` is what every later formula needs.
- **Descriptions.** "The `φ` is `ψ`", `ψ(℩x)(φx)`, is `TheIs φ ψ := ∃ b, (∀ x, φ x ↔ x = b) ∧ ψ b` (✱14·01). Why: PM defines descriptions only in use, so they are never Lean terms.
- **`E!` is unique existence.** `E!(℩x)(φx)` is `EBang φ := ∃ b, ∀ x, φ x ↔ x = b`, which is equivalent to Lean's `∃! x, φ x`. Why: that is the definiens of ✱14·02.
- **Scope of descriptions.** By default the scope is the smallest proposition, enclosed in dots or brackets, that contains the description. Of two descriptions, the first has the larger scope (✱14·113). So `∼{a = (℩x)(φx)}` and `a ≠ (℩x)(φx)` differ. Why: these are PM's rules after ✱14·03.
- **Descriptive functions.** `ψ(R'y)` is `Desc R y ψ` (✱30). A relation whose descriptive function always exists (`D`, `ᗡ`, `C`, `R⃗`, `ι`, `Nc`, `sect`, …) is `graph f` for a Lean function `f`, so `D'R` may be written `dom R`. A relation whose descriptive function can fail to exist (`ῐ`, `B`, `min_P`, `seq_P`, `lt_P`, a selection `M`) is never replaced by a function. Why: `Classical.choose` would give `R'y` a value when PM's description does not exist.
- **Existence theorems keep their content.** Where PM proves that a descriptive function exists, or what its value is (✱31·12, ✱33·11, ✱60·12, …), the statement uses `Desc`/`EDesc`, not the value function. Why: with the value function these statements would hold by `rfl`.

### Types (✱12, ✱20–✱25, ✱63–✱65; Introduction, Ch. II)

- **Typical ambiguity is polymorphism.** Every proposition is stated for arbitrary type variables `{α : Type u} {β : Type v} …`. Why: PM's ambiguous symbols assert the formula in every type.
- **Classes are `Set`.** A class of `α`-things is `A : Set α`; `ẑ(φz)` is `{z | φ z}`; a class of classes is `Set (Set α)`. The contextual Dfs ✱20·01, ✱20·07, ✱20·08 become theorems. Why: PM's classes are extensional (✱20·43), and `Set α` is `α → Prop`.
- **Relations are `α → β → Prop`.** `xRy` is `R x y`; `x̂ŷ φ(x, y)` is `fun x y => φ x y`. Relations are heterogeneous unless PM needs one type, as for `C'R`, `R²`, `R_*`, `P ⥏ α`, and every serial notion. Why: PM's relations may have referents and relata of different types.
- **PM's `⊂` is `⊆`.** `α ⊂ β` (✱22·01) is inclusion, not strict inclusion. Likewise `R ⊂̇ S` is `R ⪽ S`. Why: PM's `⊂` allows `α = β`.
- **PM's `∃!α` is `A.Nonempty`.** `∃!α` ("α has members", ✱24·03) is not Lean's `∃!`. `∃̇!R` is `rEx R`. Why: PM's `∃!` is existence of a member; Lean's `∃!` is unique existence, which is PM's `E!`.
- **Orders and reducibility.** Every Lean function is predicative: `φ!x` is `φ x`, and `(φ)` ranges over `α → Prop`. The axiom of reducibility ✱12·1 becomes provable (take `f := φ`). Why: Lean's `Prop` is impredicative, so the ramified hierarchy has no counterpart.
- **Relative types.** `t'x` (✱63·01) is defined as PM defines it, `ι'x ∪ −ι'x`, and is then `Set.univ`. The typically definite symbols `α_x`, `R_x` of ✱65 become explicit typings. Why: in Lean the type of a symbol is always definite.
- **Nonempty types.** PM assumes that every type has a member (Introduction, Ch. I; ✱10·25). Lean types may be empty. Where a proposition is false for an empty type, and only there, `[Nonempty α]` is added, and the status says so. Why: this is PM's assumption made explicit. Types of classes and relations are always inhabited, so they never need it.
- **Membership as a relation.** PM's relation `∈` (✱62·01) is `memRel`; Lean's `x ∈ A` keeps its meaning. Why: Lean's `∈` is notation, not a term.

### Cardinals and relation-numbers (Volume II, Prefatory Statement; ✱100–✱186)

The Prefatory Statement of Symbolic Conventions fixes how the types of ambiguous symbols are chosen in Parts III–V. Its conventions are part of the meaning of PM's enunciations. They are realized by the choice of type parameters, and by explicit hypotheses where a type must be adequate. A proposition that needs such a hypothesis cites the convention in its status.

- **Actual types are parameters.** `Nc'α` is `nc γ A : Set (Set γ)`, the classes of `γ`-things similar to `A`; the type `γ` is an explicit argument (PM's `Nc(ξ)'α`, ✱65·04). `μ +_c ν`, `μ ×_c ν`, `μ^ν` have their actual type implicit, fixed by the context or by an ascription. Why: a formal number is a different class in each type.
- **Assignment order.** The types of real variables are assigned first, then those of constants. A typically ambiguous constant whose type nothing fixes gets its own type parameter. Two types are never identified unless significance or a convention identifies them. Why: this is section I of the Prefatory Statement.
- **`NC` is ambiguous.** `NC β` takes the type of its converse domain as an argument, and `μ, ν ∈ NC` does not identify the types of `μ` and `ν`. Why: the Summary of Part III, Section A, and section V of the Prefatory Statement say so.
- **Convention IT** (identification of types). The logical occurrences of one formal number are in one type. Argumental occurrences (`sm''σ`) are bound to logical and attributive ones. Without argumental occurrences, equational occurrences are bound to logical ones. Bound occurrences share one type parameter. Why: this is section IV. Worked example: `pm100_511`.
- **Normal adjustment.** `Nc'α` as an argument of `+_c`, `×_c`, `^`, `−_c`, or as a side of `>`, `<`, `≥`, `≤`, is `N₀c'α` (`n₀c A`). Why: the Dfs ✱110·03·04, ✱113·04·05, ✱116·03·04, ✱117·02·03, ✱119·02·03, ✱124·021 say so.
- **Convention IIT and adequate types.** Any other formal number in an arithmetical position gets its own type parameter `W` and the hypothesis that `W` is *adequate* for it: `AdeqNc`, `AdeqAdd`, `AdeqMul`, `AdeqExp`, `AdeqSub`, `AdeqSigmaNc`, `AdeqPiNc`, `AdeqSm`. A type is adequate when the normally adjusted formal number is not null in it (Prefatory Statement, II). For a constant such as `1`, the hypothesis is that it exists in `W`; for `1` this is `[Nonempty W]`. Why: section IV says the actual type of such a formal number is one in which it exists. Worked examples: ✱110·56, with a proof that the hypotheses cannot be dropped, and ✱110·643.
- **Convention AT.** In the propositions where PM applies it (✱103·16, ✱110·71·72, ✱117, and with the Rule of Indefinite Numbers ✱126), an equation between pure arithmetical formal numbers is taken in a type adequate for both sides. It is not applied elsewhere. Why: section VI.
- **Convention Infin T.** When the hypothesis implies that some type has every inductive cardinal, an occurrence of `NC induct` whose type is otherwise free is taken at a type `X` with `InfinAx X`. Why: section VI. Worked example: ✱123·43, with a proof that the hypothesis is needed.
- **Rule of Indefinite Numbers** (✱126, Prefatory Statement, VII). A symbol for a particular inductive number is taken in a type where it is not `Λ`. Why: this is the rule PM states.
- **Relation-numbers.** The conventions IT, IIT and AT "apply to relation-numbers as to cardinals" (Summary of Part IV, Section A). `Nr'P` is `nr γ P`, with the field type `γ` explicit; adequacy is `AdeqNr`, `AdeqNrAdd`, …. Relations of which likeness is asserted are homogeneous; correlators need not be. `1̇` has no meaning in isolation, so there is no Lean term for it: `μ +̇ 1̇` is `nrAddOne β μ`, with the type of the apparent variable `x` of ✱181·02 explicit. Why: PM says the types of `x` and `P` are independent.
- **`Mult ax` and `Infin ax` are hypotheses.** `MultAx α` (✱88·03) and `InfinAx α` (✱120·03) are Prop-valued definitions. They are hypotheses exactly where PM has them, at the type PM's enunciation determines, never quantified over all types. Why: PM proves its theorems from them. `Mult ax` is a theorem of Lean (`multAx_holds`, by `Classical.choice`), but stays a hypothesis; a proposition such as `Mult ax . ≡ . p` is stated as printed. `Infin ax` is not provable: `InfinAx α` is `Infinite α` (`infinAx_iff_infinite`).
- **Series** (✱200–✱234). Serial notions take a homogeneous `P : α → α → Prop`. In Section C, `Q : β → β → Prop` is the argument-series, `P : α → α → Prop` the value-series, and the function `R : α → β → Prop` relates a value to its argument. `seq_P`, `lt_P`, `limax_P` are many-valued, so their descriptive functions are always `Desc`.

### Definitions and house rules

- **Definitions.** A Df that defines a term is a Lean `def`, and `pmN_M` states its defining equation (status `definition`). A Df that is a notational convention or a contextual definition (✱1·01, ✱9·01, ✱14·01, ✱20·01, ✱30·02, …) is a translation rule. Why: PM's contextual definitions define no object.
- **Full strength.** Every proposition is stated with exactly PM's hypotheses and at full strength. Apart from the corrections below, the only additions are `[Nonempty α]` and the type conventions above, each named in the status. Why: otherwise a proof would not be a proof of PM's proposition.
- **No trivialization.** A definition must be PM's. A theorem may not hold by `rfl` because a definition was chosen to make it so. Why: this would empty the theorem of content.
- **Axioms.** Proofs use only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. `scripts/AxiomsAudit.lean` checks every declaration.

### The Introduction

[`PrincipiaMathematica/Introduction.lean`](../PrincipiaMathematica/Introduction.lean) formalizes the mathematical claims of the Preface, the Introduction (Chapters I–III), the Summaries of Parts I and II, the Note to Section D, the Prefatory Statement and the General Note on Cardinal Correlators, in 44 unit blocks `## [intro.<slug>]`. It has three conventions of its own:
- the number of members of a class is mathlib's `Cardinal.mk`, and ordinals are mathlib's `Ordinal`;
- Lean's universe levels stand for PM's types where the text appeals to the hierarchy (Burali-Forti's contradiction, the greatest cardinal);
- a contradiction is read as the theorem that the object in question cannot exist in one type. For example, no type `α` with a membership relation `mem : α → α → Prop` has a `w` with `mem x w ↔ ¬mem x x` for all `x`. In Lean `A ∈ A` is ill-typed, which is PM's own solution.

## Corrections

Each entry is a proposition with status `proved (corrected)`: false as printed, even after the errata. The corrected statement is `pmN_M`. Where the docstring's counterexample is also proved in Lean, it is the lemma `pmN_M_printed_false`; 74 corrections have one. In 11 cases the counterexample is given in the docstring only, and two propositions use the lemma of the proposition they follow.

In both corrections and readings the intended formula is recovered from PM itself: the demonstration, the references, the comment, the Summary or a later use. The line between the two is a judgement, made proposition by proposition and explained in the status. A reading repairs a slip of notation or of transcription. A correction changes PM's mathematics: a missing hypothesis, a wrong term or a wrong conclusion. For some (✱24·431, ✱74·511) the docstring notes that the slip may be the e-text's, which cannot be decided without the page scans.

Most corrections add a hypothesis that PM's demonstration uses tacitly. The rest replace a term, a hypothesis or the conclusion.

- **Prose.** The Summaries of the numbers, sections and parts, and PM's comments, are kept in `data/principia.json`, and each unit block quotes the opening of its number's summary. Apart from the texts formalized in the Introduction file, this prose is not formalized. In the Introduction file, pure prose (motivation, philosophy, history) has no unit.
- **Demonstrations.** PM's demonstrations are not transcribed into Lean. Each proposition has a complete Lean proof, which may follow PM's demonstration or not. Corrections and readings often rest on what the demonstration uses.
- **The ramified hierarchy.** Orders of functions, the vicious-circle principle and the restriction of ✱1–✱5 to elementary propositions have no Lean counterpart. The propositions that only express them are the 17 `remark`s: ✱1·7, ✱1·71, ✱1·72, ✱9·131, ✱9·14, ✱9·15, ✱9·6, ✱9·61, ✱9·62, ✱9·63, ✱10·121, ✱10·122, ✱10·221, ✱20·631, ✱20·632, ✱21·631, ✱21·632.
- **The no-class theory.** PM's classes, relations and descriptions are incomplete symbols. Here classes and relations are Lean terms, and descriptions are explicit quantifications (`TheIs`, `Desc`). The contextual definitions become theorems; the philosophical claim that these symbols denote nothing is not formalized.
- **Indefinite chains.** Where PM writes "etc." (✱113·261, ✱125·24·25), the first links are proved.
